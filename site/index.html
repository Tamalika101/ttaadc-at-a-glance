TTAADC at a Glance

The Tripura Tribal Areas Autonomous District Council governs about two-thirds of Tripura's land and its tribal communities. This page maps the council area village by village and shows who won its seats in 2021 and 2026, how far piped drinking water has reached, and what its schools looked like when the current term began.

Method & limitations

Election. The official results portal of the Tripura State Election Commission cannot be opened from outside India, so the constituency results were compiled from Wikipedia's results table and checked against news reports (India TV, Tripura Star News, Voterlist.co.in). Only the winner and runner-up are listed for each seat. Margin in points is the winner's vote share minus the runner-up's.

Which villages are in the council area. The government's Local Government Directory (LGD) lists the council's 587 village councils and links every other village to a Gram Panchayat. A village with no Gram Panchayat is counted as wholly in the council area. A village that has Gram Panchayats but where the water records also name a council village council is counted as split. The water records give one figure per village, so split villages are shown separately rather than divided.

Zones are the council's 40 Block Advisory Committees, the level between the council and its village councils. Zone figures cover only villages wholly in the council area, so a zone made up mostly of split villages may show only a few villages. Constituency-level water figures are not shown: no public list maps villages to the 28 constituencies.

Map. Village outlines are from the SHRUG open polygons (Development Data Lab, 2011 Census villages; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), joined to the LGD village list through Census 2011 codes. Outlines carry up to about 1 km of error.

Schools. UDISE school records from India Data Portal, which lists years covered up to 2021, so they describe roughly the 2021–22 school year. Schools are placed in villages from their recorded coordinates; schools in towns or with coordinates outside any village outline are left out. "Offers secondary" means the school teaches class 9 or above.

Water over time. The Jal Jeevan Mission report keeps one snapshot per financial year. Older snapshots sometimes lack village codes; those villages are placed in the council area from the names of their local bodies.

2021 election. From Wikipedia's 2021 results page. Only the winning side per seat is used, because that page's vote counts are incomplete. "TMP alliance" means Tipra Motha with its ally INPT.

Water data are reported by state water departments to the Jal Jeevan Mission and are not independently verified. They count tap connections, not whether water flows reliably or is safe to drink. Jobs data (MGNREGA, replaced by VB-G RAM G in July 2026) are not included yet: the official portal is unreachable from outside India and data are still moving to the new scheme.
